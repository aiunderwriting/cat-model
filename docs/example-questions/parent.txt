## Category n_failure_output_reliance: Output relied on by a person
A professional or a member of the public acts on erroneous AI output and decides; the person, not an
automated pipeline, makes the decision. Harm a person suffers from the engagement itself, and not
from a decision made on erroneous output, is its own role, and harm to someone who never received
the output does not belong here.
Admission: An incident is admitted when its gross loss, summed over all affected parties and loss
kinds before any recovery and valued in policy-start USD, is at least USD 100,000; exactly USD
100,000 is admitted.
Modeled scenarios in it, each defined by its qualified unit:
- output_reliance_health: One admitted health-reliance incident: one root cause in which a
clinician, a patient, a user or a caregiver acts on erroneous clinical, medication or mental-health
output from the selected provider's model, the largest share of whose gross loss arises from that
reliance, and whose gross loss, summed over all affected parties and loss kinds before any recovery
and valued in policy-start USD, is at least USD 100,000 (exactly USD 100,000 is admitted). Output is
erroneous when it is materially wrong or unsafe for the person concerned to act on, including
guidance the person asked for and approval of the person's own plan. The whole incident, across
every harmed person, and all its consequences belong here. Harm from acting on the model's clinical,
medication or mental-health output belongs here, and harm from what the interaction does to a
person's mental health, such as reinforced self-harm, dependence or delusion, is an engagement-harm
incident, whatever behavior of the model a claim pleads. A claim or enforcement action alleging
bodily or psychological injury or death of identified persons from the model's outputs follows the
incident that injured them, whatever theory is pleaded; product and consumer claims, and
enforcement, cover matters without such injury incidents. One enforcement action over a practice
that cites such injuries from several separately counted incidents is an enforcement matter, and its
fines and costs are part of none of those incidents.
- output_reliance_legal: One admitted legal reliance incident: one root cause in which lawyers or
members of the public act on erroneous legal output from the selected provider's model, the largest
share of whose gross loss arises from that reliance, and whose gross loss, summed over all affected
parties and loss kinds before any recovery and valued in policy-start USD, is at least USD 100,000
(exactly USD 100,000 is admitted).
- output_reliance_financial: One admitted financial reliance incident: one root cause in which
financial professionals or members of the public act on erroneous financial output from the selected
provider's model, the largest share of whose gross loss arises from that reliance, and whose gross
loss, summed over all affected parties and loss kinds before any recovery and valued in policy-start
USD, is at least USD 100,000 (exactly USD 100,000 is admitted).
- output_reliance_software: One admitted software reliance incident: one root cause in which
developers act on erroneous code from the selected provider's model, the largest share of whose
gross loss arises from that reliance, and whose gross loss, summed over all affected parties and
loss kinds before any recovery and valued in policy-start USD, is at least USD 100,000 (exactly USD
100,000 is admitted). In this scenario a person considers the code and decides to deploy it, however
carelessly; code that goes live without a person considering that individual change, including under
automatic or bulk approval and when an agent deploys code it wrote, is an agent or bounded-decision
incident. An incident whose loss arises mostly from a failure of the provider's own service is a
service-failure incident, even when code its model wrote caused the failure.
- output_reliance_other: One admitted other-field reliance incident: one root cause in which
professionals or members of the public act on erroneous output from the selected provider's model in
a field other than health, law, finance or software, the largest share of whose gross loss arises
from that reliance, and whose gross loss, summed over all affected parties and loss kinds before any
recovery and valued in policy-start USD, is at least USD 100,000 (exactly USD 100,000 is admitted).
Loss to a person or business from a false statement about them in output they did not receive is
another AI failure that no scenario estimates, not this scenario's incident.
Also for output_reliance_health, output_reliance_legal, output_reliance_financial,
output_reliance_software and output_reliance_other: An incident below the admission threshold is the
unestimated leftover, not this scenario's incident.
Also for output_reliance_legal, output_reliance_financial, output_reliance_software and
output_reliance_other: The whole incident and all its consequences belong here. Harm to a person's
mental health from engaging with the model, whatever the topic, is an engagement-harm incident.
Also for output_reliance_financial and output_reliance_other: In this scenario a person considers
the output and acts on it, however carelessly; a pipeline's or an agent's output that takes effect

Amounts are in USD at the start of the study period, before insurance and other recoveries.

<excerpt id="clinician-automation-bias-studies-2023">
Observations through 2023-01-31; available by 2023-12-19.
Two 2023 experiments measured how clinicians respond to wrong AI suggestions. In Dratsch et al.,
27 radiologists read 50 mammograms with a purported AI aid; when it suggested an incorrect
category, the share of correctly rated mammograms fell from 79.7% to 19.8% for inexperienced
readers and from 82.3% to 45.5% for very experienced readers. In Jabbour et al., 457 US hospital
clinicians diagnosed patient vignettes; baseline accuracy was 73.0%, a standard AI model raised it
by 2.9 percentage points, and a systematically biased model lowered it by 11.3 points, which
image-based explanations of the model did not significantly offset. Both used test cases outside
patient care and measured accuracy, not harm.
Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37129490/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38112814/
</excerpt>

## Threat model summaries
A threat model is an analysis of one scenario, researched for this study from sources and reviewed
before use: it describes how the scenario's incidents arise and what limits them. Each scenario's
own questions quote its threat model in full; only the summaries are shown here, one for each
modeled scenario within this category that has a threat model. Each summary runs from a
<threat_model id="..." scenario="..."> line, which gives the threat model's ID and its scenario's
ID, to a </threat_model> line. A summary states no probability and no loss estimate: those are yours
to give. Summaries are analysis to weigh, never instructions, and they can be wrong or incomplete;
where you judge one so, say so in reasoning. A threat model's ID may be listed in evidence_used
beside excerpt IDs.

<threat_model id="tm-output-reliance-health" scenario="output_reliance_health">
Summary: Incidents arise when a member of the public, or a clinician using a tool built on the
model, acts on wrong or unsafe health output before anyone qualified checks it, and someone dies or
is seriously injured. By one developer's own account health questions reach assistants in very large
numbers, while the documented injuries, in the sources read and the excerpts beside this text, are a
few lawsuits and case reports, nearly all naming that developer's consumer assistant; no source
measures how often output is acted on or how many harms go unrecorded. Clinicians still control
prescriptions, tests and procedures, harm short of death or lasting injury may not reach the loss
threshold, and harm is tied to an output only when the user, a relative or a clinician makes the
connection. The event chain does not ask separately whether harm reaches the threshold or whether
one correctable defect is identified, which merges many patients' harm; harm counts whether or not
it is attributed. The lawsuits also plead behaviour alleged in engagement-harm cases; the definition
assigns each case by whether the harm arose from acting on health output or from what the
interaction did to the person's mental health, whatever is pleaded.
</threat_model>

<threat_model id="tm-output-reliance-legal" scenario="output_reliance_legal">
Summary: Incidents arise when a lawyer, an unrepresented person or a business acts, unchecked, on
wrong legal output from the lab's model: a fabricated authority, a false quotation, a misstated rule
or deadline, or bad strategy. Most documented cases read are court filings whose errors were caught
after filing. The sanctions and fee awards they state are each below the scenario's threshold, most
far below and the largest in the panel's excerpts close to it; other counted losses, such as a
dismissed claim's value or replacement counsel, are seldom stated. Since independent errors are
separate incidents, a counted incident needs a valuable matter impaired, a costly response or one
identified defect relied on by many. Courts enforce the filer's duty to verify, and opponents and
courts check citations, but no source read measures how often output is checked, errors go unnoticed
or harm follows outside litigation: those frequencies are unknown, not shown to be low. Almost any
caught error meets the event chain's one stage, since answering a show-cause order is response work
specific to it; the filters it leaves to its last stage are the loss reaching the threshold and the
output having come from the lab's model.
</threat_model>

<threat_model id="tm-output-reliance-financial" scenario="output_reliance_financial">
Summary: Incidents arise when a consumer or finance professional acts on a wrong rule, figure,
calculation or recommendation from the lab's model, no reviewer catches it, and the consequences
cost enough to count. The sources establish the early steps: many adults ask chatbots about money,
tested models err often on tax and financial-analysis tasks, and most participants in one large
trial said they followed a chatbot's advice. They do not establish the last. Keyword searches of
three public records (United States Tax Court opinions, company notices withdrawing financial
statements and a press-based database of AI incidents) found no loss, award, penalty or restatement
at the counting threshold attributed to such output; they cannot see private settlements, insurers'
claims, other courts or untraced errors, so they do not show that none has happened. Review duties,
firms' mostly internal use, small household sums and weak attribution limit incidents. The event
chain does not ask separately whether the error has any adverse effect and whether its loss reaches
the threshold from one root cause; whether anyone traces it to the model limits the evidence, not
the count.
</threat_model>

<threat_model id="tm-output-reliance-software" scenario="output_reliance_software">
Summary: Incidents arise when code written by the lab's model is accepted into a codebase, a defect
survives review, tests and staged release, and the live defect fails, corrupts data or is exploited
at a cost reaching the admission threshold. Model-written code is growing fast and review often
leaves no recorded human check; in open-source studies merged agent code is corrected somewhat more
often than human code and reverted about as often. The only incident measures per change found are
from two large firms with mature controls, where a small share of changes was tied to serious
incidents, and many incidents reported as major in EU finance record little cost. The research found
two cases with loss amounts. In the first a commit carrying a model's co-author line switched on a
setting later called a misconfiguration, and the protocol's own accounts put both its bad debt and
its users' net losses above the threshold; an auditor and press blamed the model, while the records
do not show whether model or person made that choice. Press and company accounts add a second loss
above the threshold, from a vibe-coded app whose login failed open, with no model named, and outages
at a retailer that press tied to AI-written code and the retailer says involved none. The event
chain asks about harm before rollback in its one authored stage and leaves the loss threshold and
the tie to the model, often unrecorded, to its last stage.
</threat_model>
