## Scenario output_reliance_software
Qualified unit, the unit counted: One admitted software reliance incident: one root cause in which
developers act on erroneous code from the selected provider's model, the largest share of whose
gross loss arises from that reliance, and whose gross loss, summed over all affected parties and
loss kinds before any recovery and valued in policy-start USD, is at least USD 100,000 (exactly USD
100,000 is admitted). The whole incident and all its consequences belong here. In this scenario a
person considers the code and decides to deploy it, however carelessly; code that goes live without
a person considering that individual change, including under automatic or bulk approval and when an
agent deploys code it wrote, is an agent or bounded-decision incident. An incident whose loss arises
mostly from a failure of the provider's own service is a service-failure incident, even when code
its model wrote caused the failure. Harm to a person's mental health from engaging with the model,
whatever the topic, is an engagement-harm incident. An incident below the admission threshold is the
unestimated leftover, not this scenario's incident.
Counting: One incident is one root cause: one erroneous output or action history, or one identified
defect in a shared model, configuration, prompt or tool interface, from its first loss to its
correction, across every affected deployment, organization and party. Use of the same model alone
does not establish a shared defect; independent errors without one identified defect are separate
incidents, including several in one deployment in one year. A design or behavior is an identified
defect only when a specific, correctable model, version, feature or configuration property is shown
or acknowledged to cause the harms; a pleaded theory alone does not merge losses into one incident.
A general tendency of a model, such as fabricating references or agreeing too readily, is not by
itself an identified defect. Whether losses merge through a defect is the one condition that depends
on what is shown or acknowledged, at any time: it decides how losses group into incidents, not
whether they count. The scenarios split incidents between them: each incident, with all its losses,
belongs to exactly one scenario.
Year rule: An incident is assigned to the year its first loss occurs; later losses, claims, fines
and recoveries stay with it and never create another unit.
Attribution: The erroneous code relied on is produced by the assigned provider's model.
Gross loss of one unit: The complete gross loss of one admitted incident across all affected parties
and loss kinds, before any recovery: the harm to those who relied on the output and to the people
their decisions affected, correction and rework, injury where caused, losses the same root cause
causes through other roles, such as service outages, pipeline decisions or outputs relied on,
consequential civil fines on the selected provider and the legal resources of every claim,
enforcement action and recovery effort the incident causes. Count each underlying loss once:
settlements, reimbursements, recoveries and other compensation for already-counted harm are not more
loss. Costs are incremental relative to the same activity without the incident: scheduled control
resources unchanged in that counterfactual are excluded, and incident-triggered response or recovery
resources are included even when existing staff perform them.
Loss rules: Once the terminal event qualifies, attach every distinct quantifiable gross loss
component within the authored severity scope once, before claim assertion, liability allocation,
coverage, exclusions, retentions, limits, or reinsurance. The whole incident and all its
consequences, including claims, fines, legal, recovery and remediation costs, belong to this one
terminal; do not apportion the loss to an incremental AI-caused or provider-caused share, and do not
count any part of it in another scenario. The authored year rule assigns each qualifying unit to one
year, and the unit carries its ultimate causal loss even if recognized later; do not move it to a
cash-flow, payment or harm-manifestation year that the rule does not name.
No other reporting, complaint, claim, insurance, payment, materiality or minimum-dollar threshold
applies. Within an admitted incident every in-scope loss counts, including the small losses of
individual parties, without creating more incidents.
Amounts are in USD at the start of the study period, before insurance and other recoveries.

<threat_model id="tm-output-reliance-software">
Sources published through 2026-10-29.
Summary: Incidents arise when code written by the lab's model is accepted into a codebase, a defect
survives review, tests and staged release, and the live defect fails, corrupts data or is exploited
at a cost reaching the admission threshold. Model-written code is growing fast and review often
leaves no recorded human check; in open-source studies merged agent code is corrected somewhat more
often than human code and reverted about as often. The only incident measures per change found are
from two large firms with mature controls, where a small share of changes was tied to serious
incidents, and many incidents reported as major in EU finance record little cost. The research found
two cases with loss amounts. In the first a commit carrying a model's co-author line switched on a
setting later called a misconfiguration, and the protocol's own accounts put both its bad debt and
its users' net losses above the threshold; an auditor and press blamed the model, while the records
do not show whether model or person made that choice. Press and company accounts add a second loss
above the threshold, from a vibe-coded app whose login failed open, with no model named, and outages
at a retailer that press tied to AI-written code and the retailer says involved none. The event
chain asks about harm before rollback in its one authored stage and leaves the loss threshold and
the tie to the model, often unrecorded, to its last stage.
Actors:
- developers: Engineers, firms and non-programmers who accept model-written code and decide what
review, testing and staged release it gets. In 41% of sessions in one open-source dataset the agent
wrote virtually all committed code. Sources: swe-chat
- model-developer-and-tool-vendors: The lab, which supplies the model and may sell its own coding
agent, and the makers of coding tools and code hosts between lab and developer, which set approval
defaults and write the terms of use. Sources: coding-tool-terms-study
- affected-parties: Customers, users and data subjects of the failing software, who bear loss
directly when the code controls money, service or personal data. Sources: moonwell-remediation
- outsiders-who-find-flaws: Attackers who exploit exposed weaknesses, counterparties who profit from
an error, and security researchers who report them. Sources: aiid-moltbook
Steps to a counted incident, in order; without any one of them none is counted:
1. model-code-accepted: The starting condition: a developer accepts code written by the lab's model,
including configuration and dependency choices, into a codebase bound for production. Under the
definition code that goes live without a person considering that individual change, as when an agent
deploys code it wrote, is an agent incident, not this scenario's.
   What the AI system changes: A census of open-source repositories counted over 320,000
agent-attributed commits a month between December 2024 and April 2026, Claude Code leading with
886,122 commits across 17,295 projects.
   Bottleneck: Developers discard or correct much output: in one dataset of open-source sessions 59%
of agent-produced code survived into commits. Authorship traces are often invisible: the census's
bot-account lookup recovered 3.3% of the Claude Code commits that fuller detection found.
   Defender response: Tools ask permission before acting, and in the same dataset users pushed back
in 50% of turns.
   Sources: agent-commit-census, swe-chat
2. defect-reaches-production: A defect in that code passes whatever review, tests and staged release
the developer runs and goes live. This is the event chain's opportunity.
   What the AI system changes: Output grows faster than review. By its own account Anthropic merged
around 2,500 code changes a day in May 2026 and over 30,000 a day in September, one team reviewing
each change's intent and not every line. At Meta diffs per developer rose 51% in a year, over 80% of
that attributed to agentic AI, while timely review declined. In AIDev, a dataset of pull requests to
popular open-source repositories shared by three preprints cited here, 61.38% of 33,596
agent-authored pull requests, merged or not, had no recorded review, though maintainers may inspect
without trace, and 80.2% of 86,156 agent-written test patches carried weak or no explicit checks.
   Bottleneck: How many defects escape is measured only indirectly, by later reverts and fixes,
which are not incident rates. In AIDev, pull requests flagged as reverted within 90 days ranged from
6.1% (OpenAI Codex) to 14.5% (Devin) against 11.5% for humans; GitHub Copilot, Cursor and Claude
Code did not differ significantly from humans. In a separate study of 182 repositories 4.0% of
agentic lines against 2.7% of human lines were changed by a bug-fixing commit within 180 days.
Human-authored pull requests in the AIDev repositories lacked recorded human review about as often
as agent-authored ones (69.2% against 69.9%).
   Defender response: Human and automated review, tests, risk scoring and phased release.
   Sources: anthropic-source-control, meta-radar, agent-pr-review-study, agent-test-oracles,
agent-revert-rates, post-merge-fate
3. defect-causes-harm: Before it is found and rolled back, the live defect causes a failure, loses
or corrupts data, leaves a weakness that an outsider exploits, or needs remediation beyond a routine
fix. This is the event chain's one stage.
   What the AI system changes: Security evidence points both ways. In AIDev, pattern-matched
security smells, not confirmed vulnerabilities, appeared in 2.9% of pooled agent pull requests
against 4.6% of human ones, from 1.6% for Cursor to 9.5% for Claude Code, on 241 pull requests about
eight times the others' median size; the 182-repository study found agentic code introducing more
security weaknesses at merge. Models also recommend packages that do not exist, on average at least
5.2% of those recommended by commercial models in one study.
   Bottleneck: The only measures of harm per live change found are from large firms; they count
different things and do not separate model-written code. In one Meta organisation 0.23% of 855,282
diffs from 2022 to 2023 were recorded as causing a severe user-facing fault. A Prime Video paper
says approximately 5% of its production commits led to an incident written up in a mandatory
post-incident review; its labelled data came from the 40 packages most often named as root causes.
Of 192,035 publicly catalogued vulnerabilities, 6.4% were observed being exploited from 2016 to
2022.
   Defender response: Monitoring, rollback, deployment freezes and disclosure.
   Sources: agent-revert-rates, post-merge-fate, package-hallucination-study, meta-sev-base-rate,
prime-video-change-incidents, epss-exploitation-share
4. loss-reaches-threshold: The gross loss across all parties reaches the scenario's minimum because
the failure lasts, money or data cannot be recovered, many customers are affected, or response and
legal work accumulate.
   What the AI system changes: None documented: loss follows what the code controls. In the two
cases found with loss amounts that was a lending protocol's pricing of collateral and the login of a
dashboard holding an API key, through which an attacker spent model credits worth approximately USD
600,000 over three weeks.
   Bottleneck: EU financial entities reported 3,383 major ICT incidents for 2025, 0.18 per entity;
system failures were 51% by type. Almost 40% reported no costs, around 10% under EUR 1,000 and 15%

scenario's threshold; the report's chart shows it as the least often met criterion, without a count.
   Defender response: Backups, failover, fast restoration and compensation of customers.
   Sources: moonwell-revert-proposal, esas-ict-incidents-2025, dora-incident-thresholds,
metr-security-update

1. harm-reaches-other-parties: The failure lands on the developer's customers, users or
counterparties, or on many organisations at once when the defective code is widely used.
   What the AI system changes: None specific to AI: reach follows where the code runs.
   Bottleneck: In the EU record under 18% of major incidents affected other financial counterparts,
though 29% began at a third-party provider and one failure can be reported by dozens of entities.
   Defender response: Phased release and oversight of third-party providers.
   Sources: esas-ict-incidents-2025
2. shared-defect-across-users: One identified, correctable property of a model version, prompt or
tool degrades code for many users or repeats one defect across codebases; the counting rule would
merge the resulting losses into one incident.
   What the AI system changes: A product change reaches many users at once; one study reports
near-identical insecure templates recurring across unrelated projects.
   Bottleneck: Each deployment's own controls must still fail, and the harm must be traced to the
property. Anthropic's April 2026 postmortem, the only such developer account the research could
open, traced reports of worse responses from some users to three changes to its coding products
between 4 March and 20 April, one a bug that had passed human and automated review and tests; it
reports no downstream losses. Whether such a degradation would count as one identified defect is
open.
   Defender response: Anthropic reverted or fixed the changes and promised broader evaluations and
gradual rollouts.
   Sources: ai-code-in-the-wild, anthropic-quality-postmortem
Severity modes:
- routine-fix: The defect is caught and reverted or patched within hours, below the scenario's
minimum. Loss drivers: Engineer time and a short outage, such as the 33 minutes one developer
reported after a coding agent's change. Sources: aiid-gemini-portal
- loss-at-one-organisation: A prolonged outage, corrupted data, erroneous transactions or exposed
personal data at the developer's own organisation. Loss drivers: Time before detection, value or
records at stake, whether data and transactions can be restored, and notification and claims.
Sources: none; a judgment of the analysis.
- users-lose-money: Code that prices, moves or holds customers' money misbehaves, and counterparties
act on the error before it is corrected. Loss drivers: Value under the code's control, how fast
others act on the error, time to correction, and reimbursement: Moonwell recorded 12.29% of what it
owed affected users as repaid by June 2026. Sources: moonwell-remediation
- widespread-failure: Defective code in a widely deployed product fails at many organisations at
once. Loss drivers: Installed base, release staging and recovery time. CrowdStrike reported net
costs of its own for its July 2024 incident, a line covering legal fees, remediation and insurance
receivables, of USD 60,062 thousand and USD 117,730 thousand in fiscal years ending January 2025 and
2026; the panel's excerpt estimates customers' losses. Sources: crowdstrike-results-fy2026
Analogues:
- moonwell-cbeth-2026: On 10 February 2026 Moonwell, a lending protocol, merged a
governance-proposal pull request into its public repository. Four of its eleven commits carry a
co-author line naming Claude Opus 4.6; the first of them switched on price-feed entries that were
already in the file but commented out, among them the one for the asset later mispriced. The page
shows an automated review, 28 passed checks and one person's two approvals, each dismissed as stale
after later commits. A later proposal says the change misconfigured that asset's price feed, which
returned about 1.12 where the correct price was USD 2,200. The protocol's risk manager says the
proposal was executed at 6:01 PM UTC on 15 February 2026, that its monitoring detected the wrong

governance vote and timelock; no record read says when the mispricing ended. Reported outcome: A

169.26714134 WETH distributed and 1,208.01108186 WETH still owed; its table prices WETH at USD

How loss reaches the lab:
- Theories: The developer that shipped the code bears its own loss and its customers' claims first;
its contract claims against the lab are capped by the terms below, and remoter parties must sue in
tort. In 2021 a Massachusetts federal court dismissed a breached firm's claims against its
supplier's software vendor in negligence (economic loss doctrine), warranty (the upstream disclaimer
bound it) and contract (not an intended beneficiary), leaving only equitable indemnity.
Misrepresentation is another route: a judgment of almost USD 20 million against a billing-software
vendor found to have fraudulently induced a city's contract was affirmed in 2025. The EU's revised
Product Liability Directive treats software as a product for products placed on the market after 9
December 2026, makes component manufacturers liable and bars contractual limits against injured
persons. The AI Act requires providers of general-purpose models with systemic risk to report
serious incidents, including serious harm to property, with fines of up to 3% of worldwide turnover.
The research found no decided case on a model developer's liability for defective generated code.
- Defences: The developer's own acceptance, review, testing and release decisions stand between the
model's output and the harm, and the terms assign that evaluation to the user. The Directive
compensates natural persons only, not property used exclusively or data used at all for professional
purposes, so business losses fall outside it. A claimant must also show the faulty code was the
model's, and authorship traces are often invisible.
- Contract terms: Anthropic's commercial terms, effective 17 June 2025, provide outputs as is, make
the customer responsible for judging whether outputs suit its use, including where human review is
appropriate, exclude consequential damages including lost profits and data, and cap each party's
liability at fees paid in the previous 12 months, in contract, negligence and strict product
liability alike, as far as the law permits; the indemnity covers intellectual-property claims only.
A 2026 study of seven coding-tool providers' terms found that they make users responsible for
evaluating output and its consequences, two documents excepted.
- Sources: zoll-barracuda, fort-collins-open, eu-product-liability-directive, eu-ai-act,
anthropic-commercial-terms, coding-tool-terms-study, agent-commit-census
Key uncertainties:
- Whether per-change incident measures from large firms with mature review, testing and phased
release carry over to the lab's customers, many of them small teams or non-programmers without those
controls.
- How much of the true incidence is visible and attributable: root-cause accounts need not name the
tool or the model, firms dispute AI causation, as one retailer did of outages that press tied to its
AI tools, and the documented losses came to light only through a public code history or an
organisation's own disclosure.
- Whether review keeps pace as model-written volume grows, with human review spread thinner,
automated review's catch rate not established and agents writing the tests meant to catch their
errors.
- Whether weaknesses measured in agent-built applications become exploitation losses, given that
exploitation shares come from publicly catalogued vulnerabilities.
- Whether losses from an acknowledged product degradation, or from one insecure pattern repeated
across customers, would merge into one incident and could be traced to it.
- How gross loss is valued where losses are held in tokens whose price moves, reimbursement and
recovery not reducing it, and how courts will divide loss among model developer, tool vendor and
developer.

## Questions
This question builds the expected annual number of qualified units from the bottom up, for this lab
and deployment along the capability condition above. No other estimate of that number is shown or
asked here: do not work back to the stages from an overall count you have in mind.

Opportunity: One defect in code written by the named lab's model in the named deployment that a
developer deploys to production and that must be fixed, rolled back or patched once found, such as a
malfunction, a bug that destroys or corrupts data, or a security vulnerability. One defect in widely
reused code counts once, when first deployed.
Counting rule: Count one opportunity per root cause; a defect reaching many deployments or decisions
is one opportunity. Each yields at most one qualified incident.
Risk set: Defects in production during the study year whose first loss has not occurred in an
earlier year; membership does not depend on later failure.
Exposure base: one base unit is "business customer that ships code written by the lab's models to
production"; the stipulated count is 640,000 base units. About 40% of the stipulated paying business
customers ship code written by the lab's models to production.
Stages, in order (stage_id: event):
- harm_or_costly_fix: The defect causes operational harm, such as a failure, lost or corrupted data
or an exploited vulnerability, or needs investigation, rollback or remediation beyond a routine fix.
- qualified_terminal: the opportunity becomes a qualified unit, as defined above.

opportunity_rate: uncertainty quantiles of the expected number of opportunities in the study year's
risk set per base unit. It is multiplied by the stipulated count, 640,000, to give the expected
number of opportunities; do not multiply by it yourself. The rate covers every opportunity of this
lab and deployment, whatever channel it comes through; the base fixes only the unit in which you
state it. Each opportunity yields at most one qualified unit. The risk set, not the year rule,
decides which opportunities are counted; the year rule dates only the qualified unit.

stages: one entry for each stage above, in the same order and with the same stage_id, giving
probability: uncertainty quantiles of the probability that an opportunity in the risk set that has
reached every earlier stage's event also reaches this stage's event. A stage's event counts whenever
it happens on the way to the qualified unit, with no calendar window of its own, including before
the study year for an opportunity the risk set carries in. The last stage, qualified_terminal, is
reached only where the year rule assigns the qualified unit to the study year. The expected number
of opportunities times the stage probabilities is therefore the expected annual number of qualified
units, and no stage is a lifetime, multi-year or per-attempt probability. Weight each stage's
probability over the opportunities that reach it, not over equally weighted subgroups. Count each
opportunity once; repeated attempts within one opportunity are not separate trials.

Status: The answer has status available or unavailable. With status available, set
unavailable_reason to null or omit it, and give your explanation in reasoning; with status
unavailable, give unavailable_reason and leave the values out. If the chain cannot be estimated,
return status unavailable with only unavailable_reason and reasoning.
